# ADR-003: Consequences of Staying

## Status

Proposed

## Context

ADR-000 established UPSTACK’s core design invariants, including finiteness, irreversibility, and the absence of permanent equilibrium.

ADR-001 formalized the delayed revelation of world limits.

ADR-002 defined growth as a source of cumulative systemic and moral consequence.

This ADR addresses a related but distinct axis: **the cost of continued survival after legitimacy erodes**. In particular, it defines how prolonged play under resource exhaustion alters system behavior, meaning, and available exits.

---

## Decision

UPSTACK will model **staying itself as a degrading condition**.

Continued survival under finite constraints does not merely increase difficulty; it **reduces reversibility, narrows agency, and alters the moral character of outcomes**. These effects are duration-based rather than choice-based and are not explicitly narrated.

There is no stable end-state achieved through endurance alone.

---

## Design Commitments

### 1. Survival Loses Reversibility Over Time

- Recovery systems degrade with prolonged operation under scarcity.
- Identical actions produce diminishing restorative effects.
- Some losses permanently reduce future ceilings rather than current values.

This degradation occurs due to **continued operation**, not isolated failure.

---

### 2. Resource Exhaustion Is Represented Implicitly

- Exhaustion does not trigger dramatic collapse or explicit alerts.
- Substitution replaces recovery (e.g., stabilization without renewal).
- Outputs may remain numerically valid while losing qualitative meaning.

The system continues to function even as interpretation becomes disturbing.

---

### 3. Moral Cost Is Duration-Based, Not Choice-Based

- There is no single decision that “crosses the line.”
- Harmful outcomes emerge from cumulative continuation.
- The game does not present explicit taboo choices or confirmations.

The player arrives at consequences rather than selecting them.

---

### 4. Systems Remember, Even When They Appear to Recover

- Certain penalties permanently alter future behavior, availability, or ceilings.
- Some options quietly disappear rather than failing visibly.
- Restoration never fully returns the system to its prior state.

Persistence applies to meaning, not just numbers.

---

### 5. End States Are Expulsions, Not Victories

Prolonged survival ultimately results in one of the following exit conditions:

- **Exile:** The player is removed from influence while the system persists.
- **Mutiny:** Control is revoked due to internal system response.
- **Collapse:** The system continues with minimal or absent population.

These outcomes are expected conclusions, not failures.

---

### 6. The Game Does Not Confirm the Player’s Interpretation

- No narration, scoring, or messaging explicitly labels moral transgression.
- The system does not acknowledge taboo or guilt directly.
- Meaning is inferred through downstream effects and absence.

Confirmation would reduce impact and absolve responsibility.

---

## Implications

Acceptance of this ADR implies:

- Endurance is interrogated rather than rewarded.
- Remaining longer is not inherently virtuous.
- Agency is increasingly expressed through timing of exit, not optimization.
- Player discomfort is a signal of correct systemic behavior.

---

## Non-Goals

This ADR does not define:

- Specific taboo mechanics or representations
- Explicit narrative events tied to moral degradation
- Exact thresholds for degradation or expulsion
- UI messaging strategies beyond implication

These are deferred to implementation and tuning.

---

## Consequences

If this ADR is adopted:

- Any system that allows indefinite moral neutrality violates canon.
- Any mechanic that frames prolonged survival as heroic is invalid.
- The player’s final agency is exercised by **when and how they leave**, not how long they endure.

UPSTACK models survival not as a problem to be solved, but as a condition that degrades the longer it is maintained.
