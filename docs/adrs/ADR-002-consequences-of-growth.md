# ADR-002: Consequences of Growth

**Status:** Proposed  
**Date:** 2026-01-27  
**Related:** ADR-000 (Core Design Invariants), ADR-001 (Survival as Systems Pressure)

## Context

Early design exploration revealed that the most enduring emotional impact in comparable survival systems (e.g., Dome Keeper) does not arise from explicit narrative exposition, but from the player’s lived experience of compounding consequences under finite constraints.

In particular, growth-oriented decisions (expansion, optimization, accumulation, automation) were observed to produce delayed, indirect, and often morally ambiguous outcomes that persist beyond the immediate play session.

UPSTACK aims to evoke similar post-play cognitive and emotional resonance without relying on explicit backstory, lore dumps, or cinematic exposition.

## Decision

UPSTACK will treat **growth itself as a primary source of consequence**, rather than as an unqualified positive or neutral progression.

Specifically:

- Growth actions will **increase systemic visibility, dependency, and exposure**
- Consequences of growth may be **delayed, indirect, and cumulative**
- Not all consequences will be immediately attributable to a single decision
- Growth will frequently trade short-term stability for long-term fragility

The game will **not explicitly explain** the larger historical or apocalyptic context. Instead, consequences will imply it through systemic behavior and outcomes.

## Design Principles

1. **Implication over Explanation**  
   The world’s state is inferred through mechanics, not narrated. If an explanation does not alter player behavior, it is omitted.

2. **Optimization Creates Moral Debt**  
   Efficient decisions may resolve present scarcity while deepening future risk, guilt, or instability.

3. **Finite Worlds, Finite Grace**  
   Resources, populations, and tolerance thresholds are limited. Longevity amplifies consequence rather than mastery.

4. **Attention Is a Vector**  
   Survival and growth increase attention from external systems (hostile groups, environmental pressures, internal unrest).

5. **Systems Remember**  
   The game tracks and compounds decisions. Even when forgiven mechanically, they are not erased narratively or systemically.

## Non-Goals

- Providing a canonical explanation of the apocalypse or collapse
- Moralizing player behavior via explicit judgment or scoring
- Offering purely “correct” or optimal long-term strategies
- Relieving player guilt through narrative justification

## Implications

- UI and feedback systems must surface consequences without editorializing
- Late-game difficulty escalation should be causally linked to prior growth
- Survival is not a victory state by default; endurance itself is interrogated
- “Winning” may require confronting or accepting irreversible outcomes

## Open Questions

- How are irreversible decisions surfaced without explicit warning?
- What forms of consequence are legible immediately vs. retrospectively?
- How can players meaningfully reflect on past choices without replay trivialization?
